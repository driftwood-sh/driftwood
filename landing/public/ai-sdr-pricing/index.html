What drives the price

Three pricing models, and what each one makes you pay for.

Per seat: the price tracks your headcount

How it works. Apollo is the clean example: $49 per user per month billed annually buys each rep database access, sequencing, and a dialer. The software is cheap because the motion is yours; every list built and every send is a person's time.

What to watch. Per-seat stickers assume you have the seats. A founder doing their own outbound pays for one seat; a team of five pays five times that, plus the time of whoever manages deliverability.

Per volume: the price hides in the meter

How it works. Credit systems price usage, not access. Apollo's paid plans carry a yearly credit pool per seat, 30,000 on the entry tier, spent on contact reveals and exports; Artisan meters every enrichment and send in credits; Instantly's $47 sending plan bills the lead database, CRM, and AI agents separately through a credit subscription.

What to watch. The sticker is the floor. When the pool runs out, the choice is overage credits or a tier upgrade, and unused credits expire.

Per agent: the price argues with a salary

How it works. Autonomous agents price against the SDR they claim to replace. 11x is reported near $5,000 a month per digital worker on an annual contract, and for an org replacing headcount the salary comparison holds. AiSDR's entry around $900 a month and Artisan's reported $250 make the same argument at smaller scale.

Where driftwood sits. driftwood's published plans start at $2,000 a month and are priced by demo volume and buyer needs rather than against a salary. What the money buys is demo-led outbound: a working demo of your product, built for the specific prospect receiving it, in every message, sent from your own LinkedIn or email account.

The hidden costs

What the sticker leaves out, from the vendors' own pages and the reporting on them.

Implementation fees. The sticker is not the first-year total. 11x's reported near $5,000 a month becomes a first-year total commonly between $50,000 and $60,000 once implementation fees are included, per the procurement data Vendr reports. Quote-based vendors scope implementation per customer, which is exactly why it never appears on a pricing page.

Annual lock-ins. Advertised prices are often the annual rate. Apollo's $49, $79, and $119 seats are billed-annually prices, and paying month to month costs more; 2026 reviews describe Artisan's named self-serve tiers as annually billed; 11x contracts have historically run 12 months, sometimes with a break clause at three months, and a break clause is a negotiation item, not a given. The detail lives on our Apollo page and our 11x page.

Credit meters. Credits move cost from the sticker to the usage. Apollo's credits cover contact reveals and exports, phone numbers cost several times an email, and unused credits expire; Artisan meters every enrichment and send; Clay reviews describe hundreds of dollars of credits burned during the learning period, which is why our Clay page treats the operator, not the plan, as the real cost.

The sales cycle itself. Quote-based pricing costs time before it costs money: a demo call, a scoped proposal, and a negotiation, and for six of the ten tools above that is the only way to learn the number. Budget the weeks, and get any break clause in writing.

FAQ

Four short answers.

Why do so few AI SDR companies publish pricing?

The motion is sales-led. An autonomous agent is priced against the SDR salary it claims to replace rather than against other software, a quote lets the number move per deal, and implementation is scoped per customer, which cannot sit on a pricing page. Six of the ten tools on this page publish no price at all.

The practical consequence. The public numbers that exist are procurement data and reviews, so treat them as directional and confirm on the call.

What is the cheapest AI SDR for startups?

Artisan. At around $250 a month self-serve, it is the cheapest autonomous agent entry we could verify, with the caveat that the figure is reported rather than published and usage is metered in credits. AiSDR follows at around $900 a month. driftwood's published floor is $2,000 a month.

If the need is really volume email. Instantly starts at $47 a month, though it is a sending tool, not an AI SDR.

How much does an AI SDR cost in 2026?

The agent range. Autonomous agents run from around $250 a month reported (Artisan) through around $900 (AiSDR) and from $2,000 published (driftwood) to near $5,000 a month reported plus implementation fees (11x).

Below the agents. Platforms your reps run start lower: Apollo from $49 per user per month billed annually. Six vendors in the category publish no number at all.

What do published, reported, and quote-based mean on this page?

Published means the vendor lists the price where anyone can read it. Reported means the number comes from somewhere else, procurement data such as Vendr's or 2026 reviews, and the vendor has not confirmed it. Quote-based means the vendor names tiers but no dollar amounts, so a sales call decides.

Why the labels matter. Roundups blur them, and a reported number treated as published is how budgets get surprised.
